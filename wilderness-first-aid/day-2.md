# Wilderness First Aid — Day 2 Notes

> **Disclaimer:** These are personal study notes from a Wilderness First Aid (WFA)
> course. They are a memory aid only — not a substitute for hands-on training. Always
> defer to your certified instructor and the official course materials, and follow your
> local protocols. Call 911 / activate EMS whenever it's available.

---

## Quick reference

| Item | Key number / rule |
|------|-------------------|
| **Poison Control** | **1-800-222-1222** (report when / how much / how ingested) |
| **Concussion** | Sleep OK, but **wake every 3–4 hours** |
| **START triage (RPM)** | RR **>30/min** = immediate · cap refill **>2 s** (or no radial pulse) = immediate · **can't follow commands** = immediate |
| **Splinting** | Check **pulse, motion, sensation** before *and* after; splint joint above & below the break |
| **Water rescue order** | **Reach → Throw → Row → Go** |
| **Frostbite** | **Don't thaw** if care is <2 hrs away or refreezing is possible |

## 1. Head, Brain & Spinal Injuries

### Scalp wounds
- Bleed a lot (very vascular). Apply **direct pressure** and **wrap**.

### Skull fractures
- **No direct pressure** — you don't want to push on the brain.
- Use a **donut ring bandage** around the area and wrap.
- Use **sterile dressings** (open skull = high infection risk).
- Watch for **swelling** and a **pupil that dilates first / unequal pupils** — note this
  for the doctors.

### Brain injuries / concussion
- Rising pressure inside the skull can cause **blindness, deafness, loss of speech,
  inability to swallow, nausea, and vomiting**.
- **Evacuate by carrying — do not let them walk.**
- A **concussion is a bruising of the brain.**
- **Sleep is OK, but wake them every 3–4 hours** to re-check responsiveness.

### Spinal injuries
- Signs: **numbness in the limbs or down one side**; they **may feel fine and then
  collapse**.
- **Must evacuate by carrying.**

#### Spinal clearing (assessment)
Decide whether you can "clear" the spine or must assume injury:

- **Is the patient reliable?** (sober, calm, alert, no distracting injury). **If
  unreliable, assume a spinal injury.**
- **Feel for alignment** along the spine.
- **Look for tenderness and open wounds.**
- Can they **move legs and feet without pain**?

## 2. Chest Injuries

> **Evacuate immediately for all chest injuries.**

### Rib fractures / flail chest (closed)
- Have the patient **hold something bulky against the injury themselves** (splints by
  hand, guarding).
- **Offer pain medication.**
- **Lay them down with the injured side down.**

### Open chest wound ("sucking chest wound")
- **Seal it** — tape an occlusive dressing; many tape **3 sides** so it acts as a flutter
  valve, or **all 4 sides** if you'll monitor closely. Use a **chest seal valve** if you
  have one.
- If they **develop difficulty breathing** (tension building up), **open / "burp" the
  seal** to release the trapped pressure, then reseal.

---

## 3. Abdominal & Pelvic Injuries

### Closed abdominal injuries
- **Identify that it's there and evacuate — do not let them walk.**

| Organ | Typical signs |
|-------|----------------|
| **Liver** | Signs of **shock**, bruising in the **upper-right quadrant** |
| **Kidneys** | Bruised **back/flank**, **blood in urine** |
| **Bladder** | Bruised **lower abdomen**, **blood in urine** |
| **Small intestine** | Bruised **mid-abdomen**, **blood in stool** |
| **Large intestine** | Bruised **mid-abdomen**, **blood in stool** |

### Open abdominal injuries (evisceration)
- **Do NOT push organs back inside.**
- **Keep them moist** (moist sterile dressing).
- **Evacuate — no walking.**

### Pelvic injuries
- Add **padding** and **bind** with a triangular bandage or belt.
- Lay them down with **knees bent** and **padding underneath**.
- **Treat for shock.**

## 4. Poisoning

- **Sign:** the person is **acting unusually**.
- Get them to **come with you** so you can contact 911 / get help.
- **Poison Control: 1-800-222-1222.** Be ready to report:
  - **When** it was ingested
  - **How much**
  - **How** it was ingested (swallowed, inhaled, absorbed, injected)
- **Carbon monoxide (CO):** tiredness, headache → **get to fresh air**.
- Also covered: **opioids**, **alcohol**.
- If **unconscious** → **recovery position**. If **not breathing** → **start CPR**.

---

## 5. Drug Overdose

- Give **naloxone (Narcan)** — **it won't hurt if it isn't needed** (no opioid on board).
- **Start CPR** if they aren't breathing.

---

## 6. Splinting Extremities

**Why:** to **reduce movement and limit further injury**.

**Rules:**
1. **Splint in the position found**, the **position of function**, or the **position of
   comfort**.
2. **Check pulse, motion, and sensation (PMS)** *before and after* splinting.
3. Immobilize the **joint above and below** the injured bone — or the **bone above and
   below** an injured joint.

---

## 7. Movement, Rescue & Evacuation

### Water rescue — "Reach, Throw, Row, Go"
- **Reach** with a stick.
- **Throw** a rope.
- **Row** out (boat) if you can.
- **Go** (swim) only as a last resort — entering the water is the most dangerous option.

### Ice rescue
- **Ladder, rope, stick** — extend something to them; don't walk out onto the ice.

### Electricity
- **High voltage requires a trained professional to turn it off** before you approach.

### Confined spaces
- **Only trained professionals should enter** a confined space.

### Emergency moves (to get you and the patient to safety)
- **2-person assist** — for leg injuries, one rescuer on either side.
- **Seated carry**
- **Extremity carry**
- **Firefighter's carry**
- **Shoulder drag**
- **Blanket drag**

### Stretcher / litter
- **Stabilize the injury before moving.**
- **Tarp + sticks:** Z-fold the tarp around poles.
- **Jacket litter:** turn the sleeves inside out, zip the jackets up, and run poles
  through the sleeves.

## 8. Triage (START)

- Start with **Airway, Breathing, Circulation**.
- **Use the walking wounded to help you.**
- Assess each patient on **RPM** — Respirations, Perfusion, Mental status. Remember it as
  **"30 – 2 – can you do what I ask?"**:
  - **Respirations > 30/min → RED (immediate).**
  - **Capillary refill > 2 seconds** (or no radial pulse) **→ RED (immediate).**
  - **Mental status:** "Who are you / can you move?" — **can't follow commands → RED;** can
    follow commands → **YELLOW**.

## 9. Heat-Related Illnesses

The body's **inability to regulate temperature**. Spectrum (roughly increasing severity):

- **Heat rash**
- **Heat cramps**
- **Heat exhaustion** — affects those who **don't drink enough fluids or replace enough
  electrolytes** while doing physical activity in hot environments. **Uncontrolled heat
  exhaustion can progress to heatstroke.** → **Get out of the heat and cool them down.**
- **Heatstroke** — life-threatening emergency (aggressive cooling, evacuate).
- **Heat syncope** (fainting in the heat)
- **Heat edema** (swelling)
- **Exertional hyponatremia** — too much plain water / not enough sodium during prolonged
  exertion (dangerously low blood sodium).

---

## 10. Cold-Related Emergencies

- **Hypothermia**
- **Frostnip**
- **Frostbite**

**Treatment:**
- Get the person to a **warm area**.
- **Remove wet clothing and constricting items.**
- **Do not thaw** frozen tissue if medical attention is **less than 2 hours away**, or if
  there's a **risk of refreezing** (refreezing causes far worse damage).

---

## 11. Chest Discomfort (cardiac)

- If the pain is **pinpoint**, it's a **low chance of being cardiac**.
- If it's **general pain and no position makes it better**, **treat it like a cardiac
  event**.
- If they have prescribed **nitroglycerin**, help them take it.
- **Give aspirin** unless they're severely ("deathly") allergic.
- If the **pain goes away** and they **walk slowly**, that's OK — but **stop walking if
  the pain returns**.
